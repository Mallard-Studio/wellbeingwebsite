Scrollometer Privacy Policy

Effective date: November 8, 2026

Scrollometer measures how far you scroll on your phone. This page explains what it looks at, what it keeps, and where that goes. Short version: it all stays on your phone.

What Scrollometer collects

  • Scroll distance, per app, in meters. Worked out from how many pixels the screen moved.
  • Time spent scrolling, per app.
  • Typing counts: how often and how long you type. Never what you type. Password fields and search fields are skipped completely.
  • Screen on and off times, to tell when your day starts and what you did at night.
  • Sleep times from Google's Sleep API, only if you turn it on. This needs the Physical activity permission.

Scrollometer never reads, saves or sends the text, images or anything else shown on your screen.

Why it uses the Accessibility Service

Android only tells apps how far other apps scroll through the Accessibility Service. Scrollometer uses it for that one job: measuring scroll and counting typing events. You can turn it off at any time in Settings > Accessibility, and Scrollometer stops measuring.

Where your data lives

On your phone, and nowhere else. Scrollometer does not have internet access, so it cannot send your data anywhere. There is no account, and we have no server that receives your data.

If you use Export log in the app, you choose where the file goes through Android's share menu. Nothing is sent unless you send it.

Google services

If you turn on sleep detection, Google Play services works out your sleep times on your device and gives them to Scrollometer. Google's own privacy policy covers that part: https://policies.google.com/privacy

Notifications

Scrollometer can show a midnight summary and a morning report. They are made on your phone from your own data. You can turn them off in the app's settings or in Android settings.

Deleting your data

Uninstall Scrollometer, or go to Settings > Apps > Scrollometer > Storage > Clear data. Both delete everything it has stored.

Children

Scrollometer is not made for children under 13.

Changes

Future versions may add features that work differently. When that happens we will update this page and its effective date before those versions are released.
